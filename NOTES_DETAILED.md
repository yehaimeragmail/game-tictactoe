## Game Notes — as if nobody has ever played this game

Picture a little grid, like a window with nine panes, three across and three down. Two players take turns drawing their own mark in an empty pane. One player always draws X, and the other player always draws O. The first player to get three of their own marks in a straight line wins. That straight line can go across a row, down a column, or diagonally from corner to corner. If every pane is filled and nobody has three in a line, nobody wins, and that is called a draw, or in this game a stalemate. That is the whole game, and it only takes a minute to learn and a minute to play, which is why children love it on paper and why this phone version feels so quick and calm.

When you open the game, you first see a splash screen with the name Tic Tac Toe and the words A QUIET DUEL OF WITS, then a line that says Ready! Then the main menu appears. The menu is quiet and woody, like a little Nordic table, with soft wooden colours and smooth X and O pieces that look like carved wood, chalk, stone, ink, or porcelain, depending on the marker style you choose. On the menu you will see the title Tic Tac Toe, a short line that says "A quiet duel of wits", and these buttons: Play vs Bot, Two Players, Settings, Go Pro, Share with a friend, and Rate this game. You will also see a small score area showing Draws and other lifetime tallies, and a difficulty choice with Easy, Normal, and Hard. Easy is the gentlest Bot. Normal is a sharper Bot. Hard is a Pro feature, so without Pro it is locked, and tapping it will point you to the Pro screen instead of starting a Hard game.

Play vs Bot means you play against the phone. The phone is called Bot on the board, and it takes its turn by itself after a short thinking pause, so you can watch its mark appear. Two Players means two people share this one phone, taking turns tapping. Player X taps, then hands the phone to Player O, then Player O taps, and so on. There is no online play and no second phone needed. Before you start, you can also go into Settings and change the player names. The default names are You and Bot for a Bot game, and Player 1 and Player 2 for a two-player game, but you can tap a name and type your own, for example Sara for X and Omar for O. In Settings you will also find a switch called Alternate starting player. When that switch is on, the player who starts the game swaps from game to game, so X does not always get the first move, which is fairer over many games. When it is off, check in the game which player always starts, because the menu does not spell that out in one sentence, and you should confirm it in a real game rather than guessing.

To start a game, tap Play vs Bot or Two Players. A fresh empty grid appears, nine empty squares in three rows of three. Above or beside the grid you will see whose turn it is, and the game will also show the names you chose. The board itself is plain and clear. There are no timers, no clocks ticking down, and no pieces that move after you place them. Once you place a mark, it stays in that square for the rest of the game. That is an important rule for a four-year-old to hear clearly: you cannot slide an X to a different square later, and you cannot erase an O because you changed your mind. You choose a square, you tap it, and that square is yours.

Playing goes like this, one step at a time. First, look at the whole grid and find an empty square. Second, tap that empty square. Third, your mark appears in that square, with a soft tap sound, and the turn passes to the other player. Fourth, the other player does the same thing. Fifth, after every move, the game quietly checks whether anyone has three in a line. If someone has, the game stops at once, draws a line through the three winning marks, and shows the result. In a Bot game, if you win you will see a message like Quiet Victory, and if the Bot wins you will see Bot Prevails. In a two-player game the winner's name is shown. If the board fills up with no winner, the game says Stalemate, which is this game's word for a draw. There is no extra round after a stalemate. The game is over, and you choose what to do next.

Here is a real example, move by move, with Sara as X and Omar as O. Sara starts and taps the centre square, so the middle pane now shows X. Omar taps the top left corner, so that pane shows O. Sara taps the top middle square, so the top row now reads empty, X, empty, with an O in the corner beside it. Omar taps the bottom right corner. Sara taps the bottom middle square. Now look at the middle column: top middle is X, centre is X, bottom middle is X. That is three X marks straight down the middle column, so Sara wins at once, even though there are still empty squares left on the board. The game does not wait for the board to fill up. The moment the third X lands, Sara wins. Here is a draw example with different moves. X takes top left, O takes centre, X takes top right, O takes middle left, X takes bottom left, O takes top middle, X takes middle right, O takes bottom right, and X takes bottom middle. Now every square is full, and if you check every row, every column, and both diagonals, neither X nor O has three in a line, so the game ends as a stalemate, and neither player wins or loses.

The clever part of Tic Tac Toe is learning to look for two things at once. First, look for your own chance to win: if you already have two marks in a line with one empty square left in that line, tapping that empty square wins the game at once. Second, look for the other player's chance to win: if they have two marks in a line with one empty square left, you must tap that empty square yourself to block them, or they will win on their next turn. The centre square is powerful because it belongs to four different winning lines: its row, its column, and both diagonals. Corners are powerful too, because each corner belongs to three winning lines. Edges, which are the middle squares on each side, belong to only two winning lines, so they are usually weaker first moves. A good Bot on Normal or Hard already knows all of this, which is why it is hard to beat, and why a draw against a sharp Bot is a genuinely good result, not a failure.

If you make a wrong move, the game does not punish you with a sound or a lost life, but it also does not let you take the move back. There is no Undo button on the game screen. If you tap the wrong square, that mark stays there, and the other player gets their turn. If you tap a square that already has a mark in it, nothing happens at all, and the board does not change, so an accidental tap on a full square is harmless. If you tap during the Bot's thinking pause, the game waits for the Bot, and your tap does not jump the queue. During a game you can tap the pause control. The pause panel shows Resume, Restart, Settings, and a way back to the menu. Resume carries on where you stopped. Restart clears the board and starts a brand new game with the same players. Settings from the pause panel lets you change sound and other choices without quitting. To leave a game completely, go to the pause panel and choose Menu, or use the Play Again and Menu buttons on the end screen after a win, loss, or stalemate.

Sound is simple and gentle. In Settings you will find Music, described as gentle Nordic ambience, Sound effects, described as taps, knocks, and chimes, and a Volume slider. Music is the soft background tune on the menu and during play. Sound effects are the little sounds when you tap a square, when the Bot moves, and when a game ends. You can turn either one off on its own. Turning Music off leaves the tap sounds on. Turning Sound effects off leaves the music on. Moving the Volume slider down makes everything quieter. There are no loud alarms and no voice talking over the game.

Saving is also simple. Your lifetime scores, including wins, losses, and draws, are saved on your device, so they are still there when you reopen the game. Your player names, your chosen table theme, your marker style, your Bot difficulty, and your sound choices are saved too. In Settings there is a Reset scores button, with Cancel and Reset choices, so if you want to wipe the tallies and start counting from zero, you can do that on purpose, but you should only tap Reset if you really mean it, because it clears the saved scores. The game does not need the internet to play, and your progress lives on this phone only. To exit, finish or pause a game, go back to the menu, and leave the app with your phone's normal home control. When you reopen the game, you will land on the menu again, ready for a fresh game, with your saved scores and settings waiting for you.

## What to Test — for TestFlight

Open the game and confirm the splash screen, the menu title, and the buttons Play vs Bot, Two Players, Settings, Go Pro, Share with a friend, and Rate this game all appear and open correctly. Start a Play vs Bot game on Easy and confirm you play as X or O as shown, the Bot takes its turns by itself after a short pause, and the turn indicator always names the right player. Play a full win: place three marks in a row, for example top left, top middle, and top right, and confirm the winning line is drawn and the Quiet Victory message appears, with working Play Again and Menu buttons. Then play a full loss on purpose: let the Bot complete a line, and confirm the Bot Prevails message appears and Play Again starts a fresh empty board.

Play a full draw: fill all nine squares with no three in a line, and confirm the game says Stalemate and does not declare a winner. Test the wrong-move cases: tap a square that is already full and confirm nothing changes, tap the wrong empty square on purpose and confirm there is no Undo and the mark stays, and tap during the Bot's thinking pause and confirm the game does not skip the Bot's turn. Test Restart from the pause panel and confirm the board clears completely. Test the pause panel itself: pause, tap Resume and confirm the same board returns, then pause again, open Settings, change the sound, and return to the same game.

Test Two Players with two people or by pretending to be both players: rename X to Sara and O to Omar in Settings, play a game, and confirm both names appear and the winner's name is shown correctly. Test the Alternate starting player switch on and off across two or three games, and note who starts each game, because that exact behaviour is marked check in the game in the notes. Test difficulty: play Easy, then Normal, and confirm Normal blocks and attacks more sharply than Easy, and confirm Hard is locked without Pro and opens the Pro screen, or plays as a sharper Bot if Pro is unlocked. Test sound: turn Music off, play a move, and confirm no background tune but tap sounds remain, then turn Sound effects off and confirm taps go quiet while music remains, then move the Volume slider and confirm loudness changes. Test exit and reopen: finish a game, note the saved scores, close the app completely, reopen it, and confirm the scores, names, theme, marker style, and difficulty are still there. Finally test Reset scores in Settings: tap it, tap Cancel first and confirm nothing is cleared, then tap Reset on purpose and confirm the tallies return to zero.
